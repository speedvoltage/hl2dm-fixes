HL2DM MATCHMAKING - SKILL GROUPS AND MAP RANKS
Design proposal for review - 5 November 2026

Scope: names, badge concepts, placement rules and hidden-rating behavior.
No rank implementation, matchmaking changes or image assets are included yet.
Achievement implementation is separately deferred until our own Steam AppID and
the necessary matchmaking support are available.

Approved decisions - 5 November 2026:
- Use Set A: the 18 skill groups from Recruit through Lambda Prime.
- Keep Duel and TDM ranks separate on every official competitive map.
  Each map/track pair has its own placements, hidden rating and activity record.
The numerical tuning and remaining proposed policies below are still design work.


1. THE PLAYER-FACING RULES
==========================
Required:
- Complete 10 competitive matches to receive a skill group. Matches, not wins.
- Have separate Duel and TDM skill groups for each official competitive map.
- Retain an active skill group while you have played within the last two months.
- Keep the underlying numerical rating hidden.

Working interpretation for this draft:
- The 10 placement matches are required FOR EACH MAP AND TRACK: ten Duel matches
  on Lockdown unlock its Duel rank; ten TDM matches unlock its separate TDM rank.
- The two-month activity check also applies TO EACH MAP AND TRACK. A Lockdown
  Duel match refreshes neither Overwatch Duel nor Lockdown TDM. This remains the
  proposed interpretation of the activity rule,
  rather than an additional account-wide requirement.
- Use two calendar months, not an undocumented 60-day approximation. Compare
  against the last qualifying match's completion time in UTC. If the expiry
  month lacks that day, clamp to its last day, retaining the time of day.
  Example: a match completed 5 November at 20:00 UTC expires 5 January at 20:00 UTC.
- A win, loss or draw can count toward placement and refresh activity.
- Joining a queue, spectating, casual play and community/listen-server matches
  outside official matchmaking do not count toward competitive map ranks.
- An expired rank becomes INACTIVE, not the lowest skill group. Preserve its
  hidden rating, previous badge, history and completed placements.
- Recommended return rule: finish one qualifying competitive match on that map
  in the inactive track to reactivate its badge. Do not require another 10-match
  placement sequence.
  The returning badge may differ after the newly validated result is applied.

Examples:
  Lockdown Duel: 10 / 10 placements, Vanguard, played yesterday -> active.
  Lockdown TDM: 6 / 10 placements -> unranked, four matches remaining.
  Overwatch Duel: previously Sentinel, no match in two months -> inactive.
  Playing Lockdown Duel leaves the other two records unchanged.


2. APPROVED SET A - 18 DISTINCT SKILL GROUPS
===============================================
Eighteen gives enough visual progression without making each win a new badge.
The names below deliberately avoid calling beginners weak, bad or unskilled.
These are skill groups, not levels awarded simply for time played.

Order | Name            | Original badge concept
------+-----------------+------------------------------------------------------
  01  | Recruit         | One inset chevron on a small, open shield.
  02  | Scout           | Two chevrons with a narrow central sight mark.
  03  | Pathfinder      | Three chevrons beneath a small directional diamond.
  04  | Ranger          | Closed shield with a compass-shaped center.
  05  | Operator        | The shield gains two short side bars.
  06  | Specialist      | A central hexagon joins the shield and side bars.
  07  | Veteran         | Wider shield, reinforced rim and one small star.
  08  | Striker         | A sharp central bolt and two small stars.
  09  | Vanguard        | Three stars above a forward-facing shield.
  10  | Warden          | A split defensive crest with broad shoulders.
  11  | Sentinel        | The crest gains a tall central diamond.
  12  | Elite Sentinel  | A double diamond and short, symmetrical wings.
  13  | Champion        | Compact laurel around the central crest.
  14  | Master          | Laurel, two upper rays and a refined inner shield.
  15  | Grandmaster     | Completed laurel with a three-ray crown.
  16  | Ascendant       | An upward energy arc surrounds a faceted core.
  17  | Paragon         | Symmetric radiating core with a complete outer halo.
  18  | Lambda Prime    | Original lambda-centered crest with a restrained halo.

Visual families:
  01-03: brushed steel, simple silhouettes.
  04-06: cool blue accents and a sturdier frame.
  07-09: teal accents and stars.
  10-12: violet accents and stronger central geometry.
  13-15: amber/gold accents and laurels.
  16-18: pale metal with orange energy accents and distinctive silhouettes.

The surrounding UI stays charcoal/orange. Badge colors can vary by family,
but every rank must remain distinguishable in monochrome and at small sizes.

Set A above is approved. These alternatives are retained for reference only;
they are not additional ladders and should not appear as player-selectable names.

For a more immediately legible ladder with fewer unique names to remember:

  01 Recruit I          07 Vanguard I          13 Elite I
  02 Recruit II         08 Vanguard II         14 Elite II
  03 Recruit III        09 Vanguard III        15 Elite III
  04 Operator I         10 Sentinel I          16 Ascendant

  06 Operator III       12 Sentinel III        18 Lambda Prime

The first five families share an emblem and gain one, two or three marks.

More closely tied to the game's world; progression is a fictional hierarchy,

  03 Volunteer          09 Resistance Vanguard 15 Lambda Veteran

4. PLACEMENT AND INACTIVITY FLOW
================================
Apply this flow independently to each map's Duel and TDM tracks:
Unplayed track -> Placement 0 / 10 -> Placement 1 / 10 ... 9 / 10 -> Active rank.
Active rank -> two calendar months without a qualifying match -> Inactive rank.
Inactive rank -> one qualifying match -> Active rank, recalculated from rating.

Hidden ratings update during placements, even while the badge is hidden.
Reveal the initial badge after the tenth completed placement in that map/track
is validated. Rated abandonment losses do not count as completed placements.
Ten matches are the reveal requirement, not a promise of statistical certainty.
Continue adjusting a new player's estimate more quickly for a short period.

Losing placement matches cannot reset the counter. No requirement for ten wins,
consecutive wins, ten matches in one session, or a daily placement cap.
Do not wipe partially completed placements after inactivity; retain 6 / 10,
for example, and use fresh results to refine the old estimate.

A completed match with a brief disconnect/reconnect can still qualify under the
normal reconnect policy. An abandoning player does not gain placement credit or
refresh activity by leaving. Their competitive result can still apply a loss;
rating accountability and placement/activity credit are separate decisions.
An officially resolved forfeit can count for players who stayed and completed
the match. An aborted/no-contest match counts for nobody and changes no rating.

Inactivity hides the public badge; it does not subtract rating every day or
restart the player at the beginner rating. Returning players should be matched
using their retained estimate, with more caution about its reliability.

If a map leaves the official pool, show its rank under Archived maps. Preserve
the record and last-played time. On return to the pool, an expired record uses
the one-match reactivation rule. Do not ask players to maintain an unavailable map.
Use a stable competitive map ID, so a file rename does not lose everyone's rank.
Major map redesigns need an explicit migration decision; minor fixes do not
silently reset placements or create a new ladder.


5. HIDDEN RATING - CONCRETE STARTING PROPOSAL
===========================================
Recommendation: begin with a server-owned Elo-style model driven by match
outcome and opponent strength, plus faster updates while a map rating is new.
Keep it simple enough to simulate and audit before adding individual-performance
bonuses. This is our proposed model, not a description of CS:GO/CS2 internals.

The current coordinator already has a useful baseline: 1,500 starting rating,
an Elo expected-result calculation, team-average ratings and a K value of 32.
It currently stores ratings per mode rather than per map, and returns numerical
ratings/deltas to clients. Per-map storage and truly hidden ratings therefore
require deliberate changes later; the existing implementation is not the design
specified by this document.

Proposed record key: (Steam identity, stable competitive map ID, Duel or TDM).
Store separately for each key:
  hidden rating R; rated-result count; completed-placement count; last qualifying completion;
  current badge; previous badge; placement state; rating-model version;
  references to the authoritative match-result history.

Seed a brand-new player's map/track rating at 1,500.
For a player established elsewhere in the SAME track, optionally seed a NEW map from:
  1,500 + 0.5 * (median established-map rating - 1,500), limited to 1,200-1,800.
Use only maps in that track with at least 20 qualifying matches and recent activity
as donors. Do not transfer Duel ratings into TDM or TDM ratings into Duel.
If no eligible donor exists, use 1,500. Seeding happens once; it grants no badge
or placement credit and never overwrites an existing map record.
The 0.5 blend and limits are trial parameters, not evidence-backed final values.

For a duel, use the two players' pre-match Duel ratings on that map.
For equal-sized teams, use each team's arithmetic mean of pre-match TDM ratings
on that map.
This is a baseline approximation; it does not fully model premade coordination
or a highly uneven team's strengths. Evaluate those effects before launch.

  E = 1 / (1 + 10 ^ ((opponent_rating - own_rating) / 400))
  S = 1 for a win, 0.5 for a draw, 0 for a loss
  rating_change = K * (S - E)
  new_rating = old_rating + rating_change

Trial K values, based on authoritative rated results for this map AND track:
  Before results 1-10:   K = 64   (initial estimation)
  Before results 11-20:  K = 48   (early refinement)
  From result 21 onward: K = 32   (established)
Normally the first ten results are the ten completed placements. A rated loss
from abandonment also advances the rated-result count, but not placement credit;
repeatedly abandoning cannot preserve the faster initial update rate indefinitely.

For a player returning after expiry, temporarily use at least K = 48 for their
next three rated results; never lower an existing initial K of 64. An abandoned
return match can consume this faster-update window but cannot reactivate the badge.
This is a simple adaptation rule, not a calibrated statistical uncertainty model.
Keep the same rule after a very long absence initially; assess longer rechecking
windows from actual data rather than resetting experienced players to beginners.

Every teammate uses the same pre-match team expectation and result, with their
own K based on map experience. Established teammates therefore receive the same
change, while less-established players move faster. When participants have
different K values, gains and losses need not sum to zero; monitor rating drift
and rank distribution during simulation. Do not describe this as zero-sum Elo.
Retain fractional ratings internally; rounding is not a source of extra points.

Established-player examples (K = 32):
  Equal opposition:                  win +16, draw 0, loss -16.
  Opponent/team rated 200 higher:     win about +24.3, loss about -7.7.
  Opponent/team rated 200 lower:      win about +7.7, loss about -24.3.
  A draw against stronger opposition can gain rating; against weaker opposition
  it can lose rating. Explain that principle without exposing the hidden values.

These example numbers belong in the design document, not the player interface.
Placement and return K values are starting points for testing, not permanent
promises. Repeated losses to stronger players should not imply they played well;
they simply provide less surprising evidence than losing to weaker opponents.


6. WHAT ABOUT PERSONAL PERFORMANCE?
===================================
My recommendation for the initial release: performance means consistently
achieving better match results than expected against the opposition faced.
In Duel that already provides a direct measure. In team games it is noisier,
but rewarding team results keeps winning as the common objective.

Do not initially award extra rating for raw kills, headshot percentage, accuracy,
damage totals or achievement completion. Those can favor particular weapons,
encourage farming, and undercount useful team contributions. Do not restore the
removed scoreboard columns just to support ranking.

There is a genuine limitation: players who always queue in the same team cannot
be reliably separated using team outcomes alone. Ten placement matches do not
remove that ambiguity. The first version should acknowledge this limitation.

Possible later refinement, only if validation supports it:
- Collect server-verified performance features alongside results, with a defined
  retention policy, and evaluate them against FUTURE wins on held-out matches.
- Account for map, team size, opponent strength, premade parties and match length.
- Consider useful contributions and deaths in context, rather than treating a
  high kill count as proof of skill. Do not count friendly or self-damage.
- If a contribution adjustment actually improves prediction, start with a small
  bound, such as at most 10% of the result-based rating change.
- Keep wins positive and losses negative. No rank farming through high damage
  while deliberately losing; do not add a constant bonus that creates free rating.
- Treat 10% as an experimental ceiling, not an approved feature or a complete
  performance formula. Do not invent weights before representative match data.

Research alternatives: Glicko-2 explicitly models rating uncertainty and its
growth during inactivity; a team-aware Bayesian model can represent team results
and individual uncertainty more naturally. These are candidates for comparison,
not something to bolt onto Elo by mixing incompatible equations. See sources.


7. WHEN THE VISIBLE SKILL GROUP CHANGES
=======================================
Use hidden rating bands to select the visible badge. Do not assign fixed numbers
of wins per rank: beating stronger opposition should matter more than farming
weaker opposition, and a loss can cancel some previous progress.

During placements: update rating silently and show only completed matches / 10.
After the tenth completed placement in this map/track: reveal the rating's band.
After later valid results: evaluate promotion/demotion once, at match end.
No mid-match rank changes based on a kill, death or temporary lead.

Recommended boundary buffer to prevent constant promotion/demotion:
- If the next band's boundary is B, promote when rating reaches B + 25.
- If the current band's lower boundary is L, demote below L - 25.
- Keep the current badge while within this buffer. No arbitrary promotion series
  or requirement to win a fixed number of matches after crossing the threshold.
- Apply the rule repeatedly if a large legitimate update crosses several bands;
  do not trap a clearly misplaced player behind an artificial one-rank limit.
- Initial placement and reactivation select the rating's band directly, without
  the buffer. A stale badge should not constrain a fresh reassessment.

Illustration only: if Vanguard begins at 1,600, an established Striker promotes
at 1,625. That Vanguard falls back to Striker only below 1,575. The numbers are
illustrative; actual bands and the buffer need calibration together.

Do not finalize an 18-row table of rating thresholds before there is a useful
test population. Fit trial bands from simulated and closed-test distributions,
then freeze a versioned set of thresholds for release. Avoid constantly moving
percentile thresholds that change someone's badge without them playing.
The top group should be difficult to sustain, but not reserved for exactly a
fixed number of players. Skill group and a possible future leaderboard position
are separate concepts.

Show rank-up/down only after the result has been accepted and stored. A pending
or disputed result shows Processing result, not a guessed badge animation.


8. MATCHMAKING AND OFFICIAL MAP POOL
===================================
Balance using the hidden rating for the map AND track that will actually be played.
Do not select a lobby entirely from an unrelated global rank and then pick a
map where its players have very different skill estimates.

For multi-map searches, consider each mutually selected official map with its
own rating estimates, then choose a suitable map and balanced lobby together.
Keep parties together, account for player latency, and widen the search window
gradually. Avoid unlimited skill gaps merely to force a fast match.
New and returning players use their estimates with suitable reliability handling;
never assume Unranked or Inactive means weak opposition.

Casual and community matches do not affect these competitive map ranks, even if
they use the same BSP. Official matchmaking results must come from the assigned
server for the assigned roster, map and ruleset, not an arbitrary server cvar.

Approved format split:
Use two independent rank tracks per official map: Duel and TDM. Completing ten
placements on a map in one track does not unlock the other track's rank. Results,
inactivity and reactivation in one track do not change the other.
For this proposal, eligible TDM team sizes share that map's TDM track; do not add
separate 2v2, 3v3, 4v4 and 5v5 placement requirements. Which TDM sizes enter the
official pool remains a matchmaking/ruleset decision to validate before release.

Do not present every installed/custom map as official. The coordinator should
publish the official pool and its version; examples in this document are not a
decision to approve Lockdown, Overwatch or Runoff for a particular format.


9. PRESENTATION AND ICON ART DIRECTION
======================================
Use the clarity of CS:GO/CS2 skill-group badges as a reference, with original
artwork suited to the mod. Do not copy their rank artwork or pretend we use their
undisclosed matchmaking algorithm.

Suggested places to show the rank:
- A Competitive Ranks page with Duel / TDM tabs and one card per official map
  in the selected track. Both tabs use the same approved Set A badge names.
- The relevant map's queue card and match acceptance screen.
- The post-match screen, alongside that map's updated badge.
- Player profiles and the scoreboard, using the CURRENT map and track's badge.

A map card contains: map image/name, track, badge/name, placement or activity state,
last competitive match date, and the next action if inactive/unranked.
Example: OVERWATCH / DUEL / 6 OF 10 PLACEMENT MATCHES COMPLETED.
Example: LOCKDOWN / TDM / VANGUARD / ACTIVE.
Example: RUNOFF / DUEL / INACTIVE / COMPLETE ONE DUEL MATCH TO REACTIVATE.
Show the previous inactive badge muted with an explicit Inactive label. Avoid
making inactive players look like they have never earned a rank.

No visible Elo number, exact rating delta or numeric progress bar to the next
group. Hiding those labels is insufficient if the API still returns the numbers;
send clients only the approved public rank state and placement progress.
Queue prediction internals and other players' hidden ratings stay server-side.

Badges should work at roughly 24-32 px in compact lists, 64-96 px on map cards
and 256 px in the detailed view. Master artwork can be larger/vector-based, with
clean raster exports, transparent backgrounds and consistent visual bounds.
Use bold silhouettes, restrained gradients and small details only at large sizes.
Every badge needs shape changes, not just a hue swap. Test at UI scale settings
and against both dark panels and busy in-game backgrounds.

Use brief promotion animations and a subtle optional sound. Demotion feedback
should be clear and quiet, without taunting or a loud failure effect. Respect
reduced-motion/sound preferences. Localize all rank names, state labels and help
text, with room for longer translations; stable internal IDs never depend on
the translated name.


10. VALIDATION BEFORE RELEASE
==============================
Ratings should be auditable, even if players cannot see the numbers.
Keep an append-only result/rating history and prevent duplicate submissions from
applying the same match twice. Version the formulas and rank bands so corrections
can be recomputed rather than patched with unexplained manual points.
Store the pre-match snapshots used for each calculation; never let result-arrival
order or a repeated HTTP request change the intended calculation.

Check at least:
- Exactly ten completed placements reveal the first badge, including draws.
- Only the played map AND track's rating, placement count and activity are updated.
- Duel and TDM on the same map have independent 10-match placements and inactivity.
- Optional new-map seeding uses established maps from the same track only.
- Calendar-month expiry, short months, UTC boundaries and client clock changes.
- Inactive return preserves history and reveals a recalculated badge after one
  valid match. Cancelled matches cannot reactivate it.
- Promotions/demotions around each boundary do not flicker after every result.
- Abandonment does not erase a loss or manufacture activity/placement credit.
- Party boosting, repeated opponent farming and excessive skill gaps are handled
  without treating every legitimate rematch in a small population as misconduct.
- Simulated new, returning and rapidly improving players converge reasonably;
  established players are not needlessly volatile.
- Match predictions are calibrated across maps, formats and premade sizes, and
  mixed-K updates do not create unacceptable long-term rating inflation.
- A later invalidated match can have its effects reversed consistently through
  the authoritative history, rather than blindly subtracting an old rounded delta.
- No client, ordinary community admin or local configuration can grant a rank.


11. SOURCES AND WHAT IS OUR OWN PROPOSAL
=======================================
Current project baseline:
  services/coordinator/core.py: report_result, _teams and user rating creation.
  These currently use mode-based ratings, team averages and a K=32 Elo update.
  Set A and separate Duel/TDM map ranks are approved design choices. The art,
  K schedule, boundary buffer and return policy remain proposals. None of these
  new rank features are already present in that code.

[Glicko-2 specification, Mark E. Glickman]
https://www.glicko.net/glicko/glicko2.pdf
The specification tracks a rating, rating deviation and volatility, and allows
uncertainty to grow during periods without games. It also discusses rating
periods; its suggested batch sizes are not our ten-match placement requirement.
This supports considering uncertainty-aware models, not claiming that our
proposed K schedule is mathematically equivalent to Glicko-2.

[TrueSkill ranking system, Microsoft Research]
https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/trueskill-ranking-system/
Describes a team-aware skill/uncertainty model and the difficulty of separating
individual strengths when players always compete together. It is a reference
for evaluating alternatives to team-average Elo, not evidence of CS2's algorithm.

[TrueSkill 2 research report, Microsoft Research]
https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/trueskill-2-improved-bayesian-skill-rating-system/
Explores additional evidence beyond match outcomes. Its findings do not validate
arbitrary kill/damage weights for this mod; our own match data and independent
validation would still be needed before adopting performance-based adjustments.

Set A and the separate Duel/TDM tracks are now settled. Remaining review:
confirm the proposed inactivity/return details and eligible TDM rulesets, then
simulate rating behavior before finalizing thresholds or producing badges.
